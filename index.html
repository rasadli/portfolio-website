03 Research

AI-Optimized AZN Micropayment Batching and Settlement

Ongoing senior design project, 2026–2027

When a payment is very small, the cost of settling it alone can matter more than the payment. Batching fixes that and creates a new problem: every payment waits for its batch.

My graduation project at ADA University designs an adaptive, machine-learning-based batching system for micropayment settlement in distributed environments, with a focus on payments in Azerbaijani manat (AZN). It evaluates transaction throughput, confirmation latency, settlement overhead and fault tolerance against fixed batching strategies.

When should a batch close?

A small simulation of the question behind my graduation project. Payments arrive at random, a fixed policy decides when to close a batch, and one settlement channel processes batches in order. Change the inputs and watch confirmation delay and settlement overhead pull against each other.

The model and its assumptions

Policies

Size or time
Close when the batch reaches its size limit, or when its oldest payment has waited the maximum time.
Fixed interval
Close every interval, however many payments are waiting. The size limit does not apply.
Size only
Close only when the batch is full. Payments can wait indefinitely when traffic is slow.
No batching
Settle every payment on its own. The baseline that batching is meant to improve.

Assumptions

  1. Payments arrive as a Poisson process at the chosen rate. Each run uses a fixed random seed, so the same inputs always give the same output.
  2. One settlement channel handles one batch at a time, in the order batches close. A batch of n payments takes the settlement overhead plus 0.2 ms per payment.
  3. A payment is confirmed when its batch finishes settling. Confirmation delay is the time from arrival to that moment.
  4. Each run covers 60 seconds of simulated arrivals. Batches still open at the end are not counted as confirmed.
  5. There are no failures, retries, network delays or fees in the model. Fault tolerance, which the project also studies, is out of scope here.

What the numbers mean

Mean and 95th-percentile delay
Confirmation delay for every payment in a closed batch, including batches that finish settling after arrivals stop.
Throughput
Payments whose settlement finished within the 60-second run, per second.
Overhead per payment
Settlement overhead divided by the mean batch size. Compared with no batching, it shrinks by the same factor that the batch grows.
Channel load
Time the settlement channel spends busy, divided by the length of the run. Above 100%, batches close faster than they can be settled and the backlog grows.

The project will compare an adaptive, learned policy against fixed baselines like these. That policy is not implemented here, so nothing on this page is AI-optimized.
